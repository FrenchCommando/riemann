All Pythagorean triples

Euclid, Elements X.29 (lemma 1); the first step toward Fermat
Theorem

Every solution of x2+y2=z2x^2 + y^2 = z^2x2+y2=z2 in positive integers with gcd⁡(x,y)=1\gcd(x, y) = 1gcd(x,y)=1 and yyy even is x=m2−n2,y=2mn,z=m2+n2x = m^2 - n^2, \qquad y = 2mn, \qquad z = m^2 + n^2x=m2−n2,y=2mn,z=m2+n2 for coprime integers m>n>0m \gt n \gt 0m>n>0 of opposite parity, and every such m,nm, nm,n gives one.

Pythagoras says which lengths fit a right triangle; this says exactly which whole numbers do. It's the base case of Fermat's Last Theorem: for squares there are infinitely many solutions, and the next proof uses this list to show that for fourth powers there are none.
