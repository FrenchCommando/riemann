The Riemann Hypothesis

Bernhard Riemann, 1859; a Millennium Prize problem since 2000
Theorem

Every non-trivial zero of the Riemann zeta function has real part 12\frac1221​. Equivalently,  ψ(x)=x+O(x log⁡2x)\ \psi(x) = x + O\big(\sqrt{x}\,\log^2 x\big) ψ(x)=x+O(x​log2x).

This is the destination the number-theory track has been heading for: Euclid, Euler's product, Chebyshev's bounds and von Mangoldt's formula. The analytic steps below are cited rather than proved here (they need complex analysis). The last step reports the proof announced in November 2026, what has been checked and what has not.

Roadmap, not a full proof. Steps marked cited state a major result with its source instead of proving it; the rest are worked out here.

  4. 4The hypothesis, and what it meanscited

    RH   ⟺  \iff⟺ ψ(x)=x+O(x log⁡2x)\psi(x) = x + O(\sqrt x\,\log^2 x)ψ(x)=x+O(x​log2x)   ⟺  \iff⟺ π(x)=Li⁡(x)+O(x log⁡x)\pi(x) = \operatorname{Li}(x) + O(\sqrt x\,\log x)π(x)=Li(x)+O(x​logx), where Li⁡(x)=∫2xdtlog⁡t\operatorname{Li}(x) = \int_2^x \frac{dt}{\log t}Li(x)=∫2x​logtdt​.

  5. 5The evidence before 2026cited

    Infinitely many zeros lie on the line (Hardy, 1914), a positive proportion do (Selberg, 1942), more than 40% do (Conrey, 1989), and the first 101310^{13}1013 zeros all do (Gourdon, 2004).

    Hardy 1914; Selberg 1942; Conrey 1989; Gourdon 2004

    Each result is a theorem about the zeros on the line; none excluded a zero off it. The strongest structural evidence came from function fields: for curves over finite fields the analogue of RH is a theorem.

    More detail

    The function field analogue. For a curve CCC over Fq\mathbb{F}_qFq​ there is a zeta function counting its points over Fqn\mathbb{F}_{q^n}Fqn​, and the analogue of RH says ∣#C(Fq)−(q+1)∣≤2gq|\#C(\mathbb{F}_{q}) - (q + 1)| \le 2g\sqrt q∣#C(Fq​)−(q+1)∣≤2gq​, with ggg the genus. For elliptic curves (g=1g = 1g=1) that is Hasse's bound from the Fermat roadmap. Weil proved it for all curves in the 1940s, and Deligne (1974) for all smooth projective varieties. Stepanov (1969) found an elementary proof for curves using an auxiliary polynomial that vanishes to high order at the points being counted, later simplified by Bombieri.

    Why it didn't transfer: over Fq\mathbb{F}_qFq​ there is a Frobenius map whose eigenvalues are the zeros, and geometry to control them. Over Z\mathbb{Z}Z no such structure was known.

  6. 6Status, November 2026cited

    On 20 November 2026 Levent Alpöge announced a computer-checked proof of RH, and of GRH for Dirichlet LLL-functions, written in Lean together with Claude. As of 24 November it has been machine-verified, but no human-readable proof has been published.

    Alpöge, announcement of 20 Nov 2026; Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative, joint statement of 21 Nov 2026

    The Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative certified on 21 November that the code compiles, passes independent proof checkers, and proves Mathlib's own pre-existing statement of RH (so the statement was not quietly redefined). What is missing is understanding: the formal proof is reported at about 17 million lines, and mathematicians have said they don't yet know why it works.

    More detail

    What is reported, from the sources listed below:

    • What a Lean check establishes. If the kernel and the checkers are sound, the conclusion follows from Mathlib's axioms, and the conclusion is the standard statement of RH. It does not tell anyone what the key ideas are, and it is not journal peer review.
    • The reported strategy. Two stages: a weaker, intermittent estimate for a smoothed prime-counting error is shown to imply RH by classical methods (Landau, Turán, de la Vallée Poussin), and that estimate is proved by an argument described as a descendant of Stepanov's method for curves over finite fields, transported to the integers using the theta correspondence and Arakelov theory. Terence Tao's preliminary reading was that it appears to draw on the full power of the Langlands program and arithmetic geometry.
    • Not yet available. A natural-language proof. A short sketch has been posted and criticised for its clarity.
    • The Clay Institute lists the problem as "solution claimed". Its rules require publication, a two-year wait and general acceptance before any prize.

    A note on sources: this site's walkthroughs were also written with Claude, the model involved in the proof. Everything in this step is taken from the reporting listed below, not from Claude's own knowledge, and the situation is changing day by day.

  7. 7What it unlockscited

    Hundreds of results were proved "assuming RH" or "assuming GRH". If the proof stands, they become unconditional.

    von Koch 1901; Miller 1976; Bach 1990

    Examples: π(x)=Li⁡(x)+O(xlog⁡x)\pi(x) = \operatorname{Li}(x) + O(\sqrt x\log x)π(x)=Li(x)+O(x​logx); under GRH, every composite nnn has a Miller–Rabin witness below 2log⁡2n2\log^2 n2log2n (Bach), so testing just those bases is a deterministic polynomial-time primality test (Miller).

    More detail

    Some consequences for primes:

    • Gaps: under RH, there is a prime between xxx and x+Cxlog⁡xx + C\sqrt x\log xx+Cx​logx (Cramér). Unconditionally the best known is far weaker.
    • Primes in progressions: GRH for Dirichlet LLL-functions gives the analogue of the RH error term for primes ≡a(modq)\equiv a \pmod q≡a(modq), and a bound on the least such prime.
    • Algorithms: deterministic primality testing via Miller's test (AKS, 2002, already does this unconditionally, but more slowly).

    None of this depends on how RH is proved, only on its truth, which is why a machine-checked proof matters even before anyone understands it.

Takeaway. The primes are the sum of a smooth trend and waves, one per zero of ζ\zetaζ; RH says no wave grows faster than x\sqrt xx​. In November 2026 a machine-checked proof appeared, and the work of understanding it began.
