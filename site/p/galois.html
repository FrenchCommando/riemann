Galois theory: no formula for the quintic

Évariste Galois, 1832; Abel–Ruffini theorem, Ruffini 1799 and Abel 1824
Theorem

The roots of a polynomial can be written using +,−,×,÷+, -, \times, \div+,−,×,÷ and kkk-th roots exactly when its group of symmetries is solvable. For degree 555 this fails: the roots of x5−4x+2x^5 - 4x + 2x5−4x+2 have no such formula.

There are formulas for the roots of polynomials of degree 222 (Babylon), 333 and 444 (Cardano and Ferrari, 1545). Degree 555 resisted for almost three centuries, until Ruffini and Abel showed there is no general formula. Galois found the reason: attach to each polynomial a group, the symmetries of its roots, and read off from the group whether a formula exists. Finite fields, the path here, are the case where that group is always cyclic; this roadmap goes to the general case.

Roadmap, not a full proof. Steps marked cited state a major result with its source instead of proving it; the rest are worked out here.

  1. 1Symmetries of the roots

    The Galois group of a polynomial with rational coefficients is the group of permutations of its roots that preserve every polynomial equation among the roots with rational coefficients.

    For x2−2x^2 - 2x2−2 the roots are r1=2r_1 = \sqrt 2r1​=2​ and r2=−2r_2 = -\sqrt 2r2​=−2​. The relations r1+r2=0r_1 + r_2 = 0r1​+r2​=0 and r1r2=−2r_1 r_2 = -2r1​r2​=−2 survive swapping them, and so does every other relation, so the group is {id,(1 2)}\{\mathrm{id}, (1\ 2)\}{id,(1 2)}. For a finite field the analogous group is the one in the Frobenius proof: cyclic, generated by a↦apa \mapsto a^pa↦ap.

    More detail

    Equivalently, it is the group of symmetries of the field Q(r1,…,rn)\mathbb Q(r_1, \dots, r_n)Q(r1​,…,rn​) generated by the roots: a symmetry of that field is determined by where it sends the roots, and it must send roots to roots.

    Not every permutation qualifies. For x4−1x^4 - 1x4−1, with roots 1,−1,i,−i1, -1, i, -i1,−1,i,−i, a symmetry must fix 111 and −1-1−1 because they are rational; only the swap of iii and −i-i−i (complex conjugation) remains.

  2. 2The Galois correspondencecited

    The subgroups of the Galois group correspond one to one with the fields between Q\mathbb QQ and the field generated by the roots: each intermediate field is the set of numbers fixed by one subgroup, and larger subgroups fix smaller fields.

    Galois, 1832; modern form: Dedekind, and Artin, Galois Theory, 1942

    This dictionary turns questions about fields, which are hard to see, into questions about a finite group, which can be listed. The same theorem holds for finite fields, where it can be checked by hand.

    More detail

    For a finite field Fpn\mathbb F_{p^n}Fpn​ the group is cyclic of order nnn, and a cyclic group has exactly one subgroup for each divisor ddd of nnn. So the subfields of Fpn\mathbb F_{p^n}Fpn​ are exactly the Fpd\mathbb F_{p^d}Fpd​ with d∣nd \mid nd∣n: the elements fixed by φd\varphi^dφd, that is the roots of xpd−xx^{p^d} - xxpd−x.

    For QR's field F256=F28\mathbb F_{256} = \mathbb F_{2^8}F256​=F28​: the subfields are F2\mathbb F_2F2​, F4\mathbb F_4F4​, F16\mathbb F_{16}F16​ and F256\mathbb F_{256}F256​, for d=1,2,4,8d = 1, 2, 4, 8d=1,2,4,8.

  3. 3Radicals and solvable groupscited

    The roots can be written with radicals exactly when the Galois group is solvable: it has a chain of subgroups G=G0⊇G1⊇⋯⊇Gm={e}G = G_0 \supseteq G_1 \supseteq \dots \supseteq G_m = \{e\}G=G0​⊇G1​⊇⋯⊇Gm​={e}, each normal in the one before, with every quotient Gi/Gi+1G_i / G_{i+1}Gi​/Gi+1​ abelian.

    Galois, 1832 (the memoir rejected by the Academy in 1831, published by Liouville in 1846)

    Adjoining a kkk-th root, once the kkk-th roots of unity are available, adds a step whose symmetry group is cyclic, hence abelian. A formula built from nested radicals is a tower of such steps, and by the correspondence a tower of fields is a chain of subgroups.

    More detail

    The small symmetric groups are solvable, which is why the classical formulas exist: S4⊇A4⊇V4⊇{e},V4={e,(1 2)(3 4),(1 3)(2 4),(1 4)(2 3)},S_4 \supseteq A_4 \supseteq V_4 \supseteq \{e\}, \qquad V_4 = \{e, (1\ 2)(3\ 4), (1\ 3)(2\ 4), (1\ 4)(2\ 3)\},S4​⊇A4​⊇V4​⊇{e},V4​={e,(1 2)(3 4),(1 3)(2 4),(1 4)(2 3)}, with quotients of sizes 222, 333 and 444, all abelian. Ferrari's method for the quartic first solves a "resolvent cubic", which mirrors the quotient S4/V4≅S3S_4 / V_4 \cong S_3S4​/V4​≅S3​.

  5. 5A quintic with group S₅cited

    The Galois group of x5−4x+2x^5 - 4x + 2x5−4x+2 is all of S5S_5S5​, so its roots cannot be written with radicals. In particular there is no formula for the roots of every quintic.

    Eisenstein's criterion (Schönemann 1846, Eisenstein 1850); Cauchy's theorem on groups, 1845

    It is irreducible (Eisenstein with p=2p = 2p=2), so the group moves any root to any other, and 555 divides its order; by Cauchy it contains an element of order 555, a 5-cycle. It has exactly three real roots, so complex conjugation fixes three roots and swaps two: a transposition. A 5-cycle and a transposition generate S5S_5S5​.

    A general formula, if one existed, would in particular solve this quintic, so none exists. Quintics with smaller groups, like x5−2x^5 - 2x5−2, can still be solved by radicals.

  6. 6What Galois leftcited

    Galois wrote out his theory the night before a fatal duel, in May 1832, at twenty. Its finite-field case is now in every QR code; its general case is still being extended.

    Galois, letter to Auguste Chevalier, 29 May 1832; Liouville, Journal de Mathématiques Pures et Appliquées, 1846

    The path to this destination is the finite-field half: the field of bytes, its primitive element, the Frobenius symmetries and Reed–Solomon codes, all in daily use. The general half, symmetries of roots over Q\mathbb QQ, grew into much of modern number theory, including the Langlands program.

    More detail

    An open question in the same spirit: the inverse Galois problem asks whether every finite group is the Galois group of some polynomial over Q\mathbb QQ. It is known for all solvable groups (Shafarevich, 1954) and for many others, including every SnS_nSn​ and AnA_nAn​, but not in general.

Takeaway. A formula by radicals is a tower of abelian steps, so it needs a solvable symmetry group, and S5S_5S5​ is not solvable. For finite fields the group is always cyclic, which is why their arithmetic is so tame that it can run inside every QR code.
