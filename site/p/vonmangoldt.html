On the way to The Riemann Hypothesis

How ζ\zetaζ sees the primes: −ζ′/ζ=∑Λ(n) n−s-\zeta'/\zeta = \sum \Lambda(n)\, n^{-s}−ζ′/ζ=∑Λ(n)n−s

Hans von Mangoldt, 1895; the bridge from Euler to Riemann
Theorem

Let Λ(n)=log⁡p\Lambda(n) = \log pΛ(n)=logp if n=pkn = p^kn=pk is a prime power (k≥1k \ge 1k≥1), and Λ(n)=0\Lambda(n) = 0Λ(n)=0 otherwise. For real s>1s \gt 1s>1, −ζ′(s)ζ(s)=∑n≥1Λ(n)ns.-\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)} = \sum_{n \ge 1} \frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^s}.−ζ(s)ζ′(s)​=n≥1∑​nsΛ(n)​.

Euler's product turned ζ\zetaζ into a statement about primes. Taking its logarithmic derivative turns it into a statement about the weighted prime count ψ(x)\psi(x)ψ(x), which is the form Riemann's theory uses: every zero of ζ\zetaζ becomes a pole of ζ′/ζ\zeta'/\zetaζ′/ζ, and every pole leaves a trace in ψ(x)\psi(x)ψ(x).

Takeaway. The logarithmic derivative turns Euler's product into a sum over prime powers, and turns zeros of ζ\zetaζ into poles, which is how zeros come to control primes.
