The Langlands program

Robert Langlands, letter to André Weil, January 1967
Theorem

Counting solutions of equations modulo primes (arithmetic) and the coefficients of highly symmetric functions (analysis) are two descriptions of the same objects, matched by their LLL-functions.

Wiles's modularity theorem matched elliptic curves with modular forms. That turned out to be one entry in a much larger dictionary, conjectured by Langlands, which ties together reciprocity laws, LLL-functions and symmetry. This roadmap starts with examples you can check by hand, from Gauss to a cubic whose roots are predicted by a qqq-series, then states the conjectures and what is proved.

Roadmap, not a full proof. Steps marked cited state a major result with its source instead of proving it; the rest are worked out here.

Builds on Lagrange's theorem, Infinitely many primes ≡3(mod4)\equiv 3 \pmod 4≡3(mod4), Fermat's Last Theorem.

  1. 1A first reciprocity lawcited

    For a prime p≠2,5p \ne 2, 5p=2,5:  x2≡5(modp)\ x^2 \equiv 5 \pmod p x2≡5(modp) has a solution exactly when p≡±1(mod5)p \equiv \pm 1 \pmod 5p≡±1(mod5).

    Quadratic reciprocity: conjectured by Euler and Legendre, proved by Gauss, 1796

    Quadratic reciprocity says that for odd primes p,qp, qp,q with q≡1(mod4)q \equiv 1 \pmod 4q≡1(mod4), qqq is a square mod ppp exactly when ppp is a square mod qqq. With q=5q = 5q=5: the question becomes whether ppp is a square mod 555, and the squares mod 5 are 111 and 4≡−14 \equiv -14≡−1.

    More detail

    Why this is surprising: "is 555 a square mod ppp?" is a question about arithmetic mod ppp, which changes completely from one prime to the next. The answer depends only on ppp mod 5, a fixed, small modulus. Examples: p=11≡1p = 11 \equiv 1p=11≡1: 42=16≡54^2 = 16 \equiv 542=16≡5 ✓. p=19≡−1p = 19 \equiv -1p=19≡−1: 92=81=76+59^2 = 81 = 76 + 592=81=76+5 ✓. p=7≡2p = 7 \equiv 2p=7≡2: squares mod 7 are 1,2,41, 2, 41,2,4, no 555.

    A rule of this shape, where how a polynomial factors mod ppp is decided by a congruence condition on ppp, is called a reciprocity law. Gauss found eight proofs of this one; the search for higher reciprocity laws drove algebraic number theory for the next 150 years.

  2. 2When congruences are enoughcited

    If the symmetry group of a polynomial's roots is abelian, how it factors mod ppp depends only on ppp mod some fixed NNN.

    Kronecker–Weber theorem (Kronecker 1853, Weber 1886, Hilbert 1896); class field theory (Takagi 1920, Artin 1927)

    The roots of a polynomial over Q\mathbb QQ are permuted by its Galois group. When that group is abelian, Kronecker–Weber puts the roots inside a field generated by roots of unity, and for roots of unity the rule is visible: xn−1x^n - 1xn−1 splits into nnn distinct roots mod ppp exactly when p≡1(modn)p \equiv 1 \pmod np≡1(modn).

    More detail

    The roots-of-unity rule is group theory: the nonzero residues mod ppp form a cyclic group of order p−1p - 1p−1 (there is a primitive root), and a cyclic group of order mmm has nnn elements with xn=1x^n = 1xn=1 exactly when n∣mn \mid mn∣m. One direction is Lagrange's theorem: the order of each root of xn−1x^n - 1xn−1 divides both nnn and p−1p - 1p−1.

    Class field theory is the full abelian reciprocity law: every abelian Galois group is described by congruence conditions. In Langlands's language, these conditions are characters χ:(Z/N)×→C×\chi : (\mathbb Z/N)^\times \to \mathbb C^\timesχ:(Z/N)×→C×, the "automorphic forms for GL1\mathrm{GL}_1GL1​", and their LLL-functions ∑χ(n)n−s\sum \chi(n) n^{-s}∑χ(n)n−s are Dirichlet's, the same ones from the primes-in-progressions proof.

  3. 3When they are not: a cubic and a q-seriescited

    For every prime p≠23p \ne 23p=23, the number of roots of x3−x−1x^3 - x - 1x3−x−1 mod ppp equals ap+1a_p + 1ap​+1, where ∑anqn=q∏n≥1(1−qn)(1−q23n)=q−q2−q3+q6+⋯\displaystyle \sum a_n q^n = q\prod_{n \ge 1}(1 - q^n)(1 - q^{23n}) = q - q^2 - q^3 + q^6 + \cdots∑an​qn=qn≥1∏​(1−qn)(1−q23n)=q−q2−q3+q6+⋯

    Hecke; the example as presented in Serre, "On a theorem of Jordan", Bull. AMS 2003

    The Galois group of x3−x−1x^3 - x - 1x3−x−1 is S3S_3S3​, which is not abelian, so no congruence rule on ppp can decide how it factors. Yet the product, a modular form of weight 1 and level 23, predicts it prime by prime. Check p=5p = 5p=5: x=2x = 2x=2 gives 8−2−1=5≡08 - 2 - 1 = 5 \equiv 08−2−1=5≡0, and it is the only root, matching a5=0a_5 = 0a5​=0.

    More detail

    Check p=2p = 2p=2: x3−x−1x^3 - x - 1x3−x−1 is 111 at both x=0x = 0x=0 and x=1x = 1x=1, so no roots; and expanding the product, q(1−q)(1−q2)⋯=q−q2−⋯q(1 - q)(1 - q^2)\cdots = q - q^2 - \cdotsq(1−q)(1−q2)⋯=q−q2−⋯ gives a2=−1a_2 = -1a2​=−1. ✓

    What a congruence can't see, a modular form can. Langlands's insight was that this is the general pattern: non-abelian reciprocity laws are given by automorphic forms. The level 232323 is not an accident: the discriminant of x3−x−1x^3 - x - 1x3−x−1 is −23-23−23.

  4. 4Elliptic curves are the GL₂ casecited

    Modularity (every elliptic curve over Q\mathbb QQ matches a weight-2 modular form) is the same kind of statement, one dimension up: a 2-dimensional Galois representation matches an automorphic form for GL2\mathrm{GL}_2GL2​.

    Wiles; Taylor–Wiles 1995; Breuil–Conrad–Diamond–Taylor 2001; Khare–Wintenberger 2009 (Serre's conjecture)

    The Galois group acts on the points of order ℓk\ell^kℓk of an elliptic curve, a 2-dimensional representation. The trace of the "Frobenius at ppp" in it is exactly ap=p+1−#E(Fp)a_p = p + 1 - \#E(\mathbb F_p)ap​=p+1−#E(Fp​), the point count from the Fermat roadmap, and modularity says those traces are a modular form's coefficients.

    More detail

    So the cubic in the previous step and the curves in Wiles's theorem are the same phenomenon: a representation of the Galois group whose Frobenius traces (root counts, point counts) are the coefficients of an automorphic form.

    Wiles's own starting point was a piece of functoriality: the Langlands–Tunnell theorem, which makes the mod-3 representation of the curve modular. Khare and Wintenberger later proved Serre's modularity conjecture: every odd, irreducible 2-dimensional mod-ppp representation comes from a modular form.

  5. 5Langlands's conjecturescited

    Reciprocity: nnn-dimensional Galois representations correspond to automorphic representations of GLn\mathrm{GL}_nGLn​, with equal LLL-functions. Functoriality: a map between the "dual groups" of two groups carries automorphic forms of one to automorphic forms of the other.

    Langlands, letter to Weil (1967) and "Problems in the theory of automorphic forms" (1970)

    On both sides the LLL-function is an Euler product ∏p(local factor at p)−1\prod_p (\text{local factor at } p)^{-1}∏p​(local factor at p)−1. On the Galois side the local factor comes from Frobenius at ppp; on the automorphic side, from the Hecke eigenvalues at ppp. "Same LLL-function" means the local factors agree at almost every prime.

    More detail

    The 17-page handwritten letter asked Weil to read it as pure speculation, adding that if he couldn't, he surely had a wastebasket handy. Weil never replied, but had it typed, and the typed copy circulated among specialists. A scan is in the Institute for Advanced Study's Langlands archive (sources below).

    The Riemann zeta function is the simplest entry: the trivial 1-dimensional representation matches the constant automorphic form on GL1\mathrm{GL}_1GL1​, and its LLL-function is ζ(s)=∏p(1−p−s)−1\zeta(s) = \prod_p (1 - p^{-s})^{-1}ζ(s)=∏p​(1−p−s)−1. Dirichlet LLL-functions are the other GL1\mathrm{GL}_1GL1​ entries, and modular forms the GL2\mathrm{GL}_2GL2​ entries.

    A payoff Langlands had in view: Artin's conjecture, that the LLL-function of every non-trivial irreducible Galois representation is entire. Automorphic LLL-functions are known to be entire, so reciprocity would prove it.

  6. 6A consequence you can see: Sato–Tatecited

    For an elliptic curve over Q\mathbb QQ without complex multiplication, the numbers ap/2p∈[−1,1]a_p / 2\sqrt p \in [-1, 1]ap​/2p​∈[−1,1] are distributed like the semicircle 2π1−t2\frac{2}{\pi}\sqrt{1 - t^2}π2​1−t2​.

    Conjectured by Sato and Tate, 1960s; proved by Clozel, Harris, Shepherd-Barron and Taylor (2008) and Barnet-Lamb, Geraghty, Harris and Taylor (2011)

    Hasse's bound puts every ap/2pa_p/2\sqrt pap​/2p​ in [−1,1][-1, 1][−1,1]. The distribution inside that interval is controlled by the symmetric-power LLL-functions of the curve, and the proof shows they are automorphic: a case of functoriality (GL2→GLn+1\mathrm{GL}_2 \to \mathrm{GL}_{n+1}GL2​→GLn+1​), proved "potentially" by Taylor and collaborators.

    More detail

    Write ap=2pcos⁡θpa_p = 2\sqrt p\cos\theta_pap​=2p​cosθp​. Sato–Tate says the angles θp\theta_pθp​ follow the density 2πsin⁡2θ\frac{2}{\pi}\sin^2\thetaπ2​sin2θ on [0,π][0, \pi][0,π], which is the semicircle in t=cos⁡θt = \cos\thetat=cosθ. It's the distribution of the trace of a random rotation in SU(2)\mathrm{SU}(2)SU(2), and that is not a coincidence: SU(2)\mathrm{SU}(2)SU(2) is the symmetry group the Langlands picture attaches to a non-CM curve.

    The step from "each symmetric power LLL-function is analytic and non-zero on Re⁡s=1\operatorname{Re} s = 1Res=1" to "the angles are equidistributed" is the same argument as the Prime Number Theorem, one LLL-function at a time.

  7. 7What is proved, and where it leadscited

    Proved: GL1\mathrm{GL}_1GL1​ (class field theory), much of GL2\mathrm{GL}_2GL2​ over Q\mathbb QQ, the function-field case of GLn\mathrm{GL}_nGLn​, the fundamental lemma, and geometric Langlands. Open: functoriality and reciprocity in general.

    Drinfeld (GL₂ over function fields); L. Lafforgue, Fields Medal 2002 (GLₙ); Ngô, Fields Medal 2010 (fundamental lemma); Gaitsgory, Raskin and collaborators, 2024 (geometric Langlands)

    Over function fields (curves over Fq\mathbb F_qFq​), Drinfeld and Lafforgue proved reciprocity for GLn\mathrm{GL}_nGLn​ using moduli spaces of "shtukas". Ngô's fundamental lemma removed a long-standing obstacle in the trace formula, Langlands's main tool for functoriality. The geometric version, where numbers are replaced by functions on a complex curve, was proved in a series of papers announced in 2024.

    More detail

    What remains open over Q\mathbb QQ: reciprocity in dimension ≥3\ge 3≥3 in general, functoriality beyond special cases, and Artin's conjecture in general (known for many 2-dimensional cases).

    And the Riemann Hypothesis. The generalised Riemann Hypothesis is expected for every automorphic LLL-function, the whole right-hand column of the dictionary. Reporting on the November 2026 Lean proof of RH (see the Riemann Hypothesis roadmap) quotes Terence Tao's preliminary reading that it appears to use the full power of modern developments on the Langlands program and arithmetic geometry. If that holds up, the program that began with Gauss's reciprocity law will have supplied the tools for Riemann's problem.

Takeaway. Reciprocity laws say that how equations behave mod ppp is governed by analysis. Langlands conjectured one dictionary for all of them, organised by LLL-functions and symmetry groups; Fermat's Last Theorem and Sato–Tate are entries already proved.
