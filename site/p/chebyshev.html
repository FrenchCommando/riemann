Theorem

Let π(x)\pi(x)π(x) be the number of primes ≤x\le x≤x. For every n≥1n \ge 1n≥1, π(2n)  ≥  log⁡2⋅2nlog⁡2n−2.\pi(2n) \;\ge\; \log 2 \cdot \frac{2n}{\log 2n} - 2.π(2n)≥log2⋅log2n2n​−2.

Euclid: infinitely many primes. Euler: ∑1/p\sum 1/p∑1/p diverges. Chebyshev: π(x)\pi(x)π(x) is at least a constant times x/log⁡xx/\log xx/logx — the true order of growth, as the Prime Number Theorem confirms. The proof studies one number: the central binomial coefficient (2nn)\binom{2n}{n}(n2n​).
