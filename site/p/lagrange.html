Lagrange's theorem

Lagrange, 1771 (in germ); general form Jordan, 1861
Theorem

If HHH is a subgroup of a finite group GGG, then ∣H∣|H|∣H∣ divides ∣G∣|G|∣G∣. Corollary (Euler): aφ(n)≡1(modn)a^{\varphi(n)} \equiv 1 \pmod naφ(n)≡1(modn) whenever gcd⁡(a,n)=1\gcd(a, n) = 1gcd(a,n)=1.

The first theorem of group theory, and a second, more conceptual proof of Fermat's Little Theorem: ap−1≡1a^{p-1} \equiv 1ap−1≡1 because the order of aaa must divide the size of the group.

Takeaway. Partition into equal pieces, then count: divisibility falls out of a counting argument.
